# Descriptive Statistics

> **Week 11 · Module 03 · Statistics and Math for ML**

Summarize datasets with measures of center, spread, position, and distribution shape.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to:

- explain the purpose of descriptive statistics in your own words;
- recognize the main concepts and decisions involved;
- apply the topic in a focused practical activity;
- identify common mistakes and validate your work.

- **mean, median, and mode** — connect this idea to the chapter goal and identify when it is useful.

- **outliers** — connect this idea to the chapter goal and identify when it is useful.
- **descriptive summaries** — connect this idea to the chapter goal and identify when it is useful.

## How to study this chapter

1. Begin with the problem this topic solves.
2. Learn the vocabulary in the context of a small example.
3. Work through the process by hand or with a tiny dataset.
4. Implement the smallest useful version.
5. Change one input and predict the result before running it.
6. Record what worked, what failed, and why.

## Practical activity

Compare two groups whose means are similar but whose variability is very different.

Before you finish, write down the input, expected output, assumptions, and at least two checks that would reveal an incorrect result.

## Common mistakes

- Reporting a number without context.
- Ignoring method assumptions.
- Treating correlation as proof of causation.

## Key takeaways

- Descriptive Statistics belongs to a larger workflow; understand both the technique and where it fits.
- Small, testable examples make assumptions and errors easier to see.
- A trustworthy result includes validation, interpretation, and clearly stated limitations.
